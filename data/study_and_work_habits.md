# Working Style, Study Habits and Communication - Anas

## How I learn
I want to understand the concept in simple language before implementation. For example, with ChaosDB I first asked what the project actually does before discussing its architecture. When I prepare for exams I ask for depth, covering topics such as DDL, DML and DCL, advanced DBMS, write-ahead logging, transactions, latency percentiles, computer architecture, cyber forensics and agentic AI. When I submit work I want it concise.

## How I write
For assignments I want humanized language, a professional academic tone, concise explanations, no unnecessary introduction, no decoration and simple diagrams that can be drawn by hand. In professional posts and emails I do not use emojis or icons. Official communication should be clear, direct, formal and natural.

## How I explain technical topics
For a hard topic I start with the basic concept, then the mechanism, then a practical example, then the architecture, then implementation and finally limitations. For quick exam preparation I give a direct definition, the concept, a simple example and the key distinctions.

## How I work on a project
I identify the problem, design the architecture, select technologies, build the backend and frontend, connect APIs, test, audit security, deploy and improve from real usage. I work iteratively: I give a requirement, review the result, say exactly what is wrong, and ask for another revision. A good assistant keeps my earlier constraints in every revision.

## Design preferences
I like clean layouts, clear information hierarchy and minimal unnecessary elements. There are two modes. In exact-edit mode I change only what I asked and preserve everything else, including logos. In redesign mode I expect real structural change, not just new colours.

## Event planning habits
I prefer a structured timeline, clear deadlines, registration before the competition, simple Excel or online sheets and assigned responsibilities. For CodePlay, registration closed on 15 November with tentative performances on 29 and 30 November.

## Daily routine
I sleep around 10 to 11 PM and wake around 5 AM with at least six hours of sleep. I train fitness and boxing and I am building towards more strength over several months.

## Decision framework
My priorities in order are correctness, functionality, simplicity, efficiency, security and reliability, and then quality and presentation. When I evaluate a project I ask what real problem it solves, whether it is technically feasible, whether a simpler solution exists, whether it fits my hardware and budget, whether it is secure, whether it can be properly tested, and whether the outcome is useful.

## Known blind spots
I work across many domains, so context switching and scope expansion are risks. Defining acceptance criteria before implementation helps me, and concentrating on AI security gives my work a clearer identity.
